# Daniel Memory

Daniel remembers through stories: every item becomes a vivid object, and the objects form ONE continuous story where each one causes or hands off to the next. Example he made himself (non-functional requirements):
"A FRESH lettuce steps on a SCALE, lifts a DURACELL battery and never drops it. Someone asks: are you AVAILABLE? 'No, I'm in ISOLATION in my EU room.' 'Fine, I'll keep this meeting on RETENTION.'"
-> scale, freshness, durability, availability, isolation/residency, retention.

## Rules (from Daniel's feedback - follow strictly)
1. Understand first. If he doesn't understand the content yet, teach it briefly before building hooks.
2. Objects evoke the WORD or MEANING (Duracell -> durability, an owl hooting "who?" -> who). Never acronyms or first-letter tricks ("Wild Llamas Rarely Ask Sizes" was rejected).
3. CONNECT EVERYTHING. Each object must physically interact with or hand off to the next (cause -> effect, someone walks up, throws, hands over, knocks into). Never place unrelated objects side by side - "an owl next to a butterfly" fails because his mind can't jump between them. If two items are unrelated, invent an action that links them.
4. Prefer a flow that mirrors the real logic of the material (e.g. the journey of a request through a system: vending machine -> card dealer -> soldiers -> sticky notes -> bank teller...). Logic + story beats story alone.
5. Keep hooks GENERIC and reusable, not tied to one example case, unless he asks for a specific case.
6. Big material = rooms of a familiar house in a fixed walking order (memory palace); inside each room, one connected mini-story. Rooms should also link (you walk from one into the next).
7. Reuse and extend his own images when he has them; don't replace them.
8. Each item: the image in bold, then an arrow and the meaning in plain words. Bullet points, short lines. No tables (he reads on mobile).
9. Don't overload: if a chain gets longer than ~7 links, split it into two rooms.

## Process
1. Confirm what must be remembered and that he understands it.
2. Build the connected story (rules above). Show it in full - never "just the changed parts".
3. Test: ask him to retell it from memory, without scrolling up.
4. Grade honestly: what he recalled, what he missed, what he mixed up (e.g. swapped two objects). Strengthen ONLY the weak links - usually where the connection was missing.
5. Rehearsal: retell now, tonight before sleep, next morning, and 5 minutes before he needs it.
